Andrej Karpathy coined the term "vibe coding" — that flow state where you describe what you want and an AI writes the code. You don't scrutinize every line. You vibe. You iterate. You ship.

It's a great term. But it describes maybe 20% of what it takes to build a company.

What about the other 80%?

## Code Is the Easy Part

Here's a dirty secret about startups: writing code was never the hardest part. The hardest part is everything *around* the code. The docs nobody wants to write. The trademark filings you keep postponing. The blog post that's been "almost done" for three weeks. The npm package that needs a README, a LICENSE, a proper `package.json`, CI/CD, and a changelog. The design system. The domain registrations. The social media accounts. The contributor guidelines. The security scanner you promised in your roadmap.

Every founder knows this list. It's the graveyard of good intentions — the stuff that separates a side project from a real company.

For the past two weeks, I've been running an experiment. I didn't just vibe code. I vibe *founded*.

## What Vibe Founding Looks Like

I'm building [ClawSouls](https://clawsouls.org) — an open platform for AI agent personas built on a spec called Soul Spec. Think of it as a portable identity layer for AI agents: personality, voice, ethics, memory — all defined in a JSON + Markdown package that any framework can consume.

Here's what two weeks of vibe founding produced:

- **The platform itself** — schema, validator, CLI, MCP server, web editor
- **80+ published souls** in the public registry
- **7 npm packages** — published, documented, versioned
- **3 trademark filings** — searched, drafted, submitted
- **5+ domains** — claimed and configured
- **A docs site, a blog, and a landing page**
- **SoulScan** — a security scanner for soul files
- **GitHub org** with repos, issues, and project boards
- **Social media presence** across platforms
- **Academic papers** deposited on Zenodo
- **Blog posts crossposted** to Hashnode and Medium

A small team, amplified by AI. Two weeks.

My AI development partner runs on [OpenClaw](https://openclaw.ai). It doesn't just write code — it writes docs, drafts trademark applications, sets up CI pipelines, publishes blog posts, configures DNS, and reviews security policies.

It doesn't context-switch. It doesn't say "I'll get to it next sprint." It executes while you sleep.

## The Bottleneck Was Never Ideas

Every founder I know has a backlog that could fill three lifetimes. Ideas aren't scarce. Execution bandwidth is.

Before AI, you had three options:
1. **Do it yourself** — and watch your to-do list grow faster than you can shrink it
2. **Hire a team** — and spend half your time managing, aligning, and onboarding
3. **Don't do it** — and accept that your startup will look half-baked for months

Vibe founding unlocks a fourth option: **ship all of it, now.**

Not in a sloppy, move-fast-and-break-things way. In a thorough, every-detail-handled way. The trademark search gets done *and* the README gets polished *and* the blog post goes live *and* the security scanner catches edge cases — all in the same afternoon.

This isn't about replacing teams. Every team member brings vision, taste, and judgment that no AI can replicate. But the sheer volume of *execution* — the 200 tasks between "we should do this" and "it's done" — that's where AI amplifies a small team to 10x output.

## The Evolution

Karpathy gave us vibe coding. But the natural progression is bigger:

**Vibe coding** → write software by vibing with AI.
**Vibe designing** → iterate on UX, logos, and design systems with AI.
**Vibe marketing** → draft copy, blog posts, social media strategies with AI.
**Vibe founding** → build and operate an entire startup with AI as your execution partner.

Each step up the ladder isn't just about a new domain. It's about a new *mindset*. Vibe founding means you stop thinking of AI as a tool for one task and start thinking of it as a co-founder who handles the breadth of execution while you focus on direction.

## What This Means for Solo Founders

If you're a solo founder — or a tiny team — this changes your calculus completely.

You no longer need to choose between "ship the product" and "set up proper infrastructure." You don't need to apologize for not having a marketing site yet. You don't need to explain why the docs are sparse.

You can operate at team scale from day one.

That doesn't mean AI makes everything perfect. It doesn't. You still need taste. You still need to make hard decisions about what to build and what to skip. You still need to talk to users and understand markets. The strategic thinking is still yours.

But the execution gap — that brutal distance between deciding to do something and actually getting it done — just collapsed.

## The Name Matters

I'm calling this "vibe founding" because the vibe part is important. It's not about micromanaging an AI through a checklist. It's about developing a working rhythm — a shared context, a shorthand, a trust loop. You describe the outcome. Your AI partner figures out the path. You review, adjust, iterate.

It feels less like delegating and more like *jamming*. You're riffing off each other. The founder brings the vision and the taste; the AI brings the bandwidth and the tirelessness.

When it clicks, it doesn't feel like you're using a tool. It feels like you have a partner who just happens to be available 24/7 and never drops a thread.

## Honest Limitations

This is an N=1 case study. It deserves the same caveats as any single-case observation:

- **The founding team had significant technical backgrounds.** This is a major confound. Vibe founding with AI requires knowing what good output looks like — you need taste and judgment to review what the AI produces. A non-technical founder would have a very different experience.
- **Two weeks captures creation, not survival.** Building a platform is the easy part. Achieving product-market fit, retaining users, and generating revenue are harder problems that AI doesn't magically solve.
- **We don't claim AI "solves" startup creation.** Strategic decisions — what to build, who to serve, when to pivot — remained entirely human. AI amplified execution, not judgment.
- **Survivorship bias applies.** You're reading this because it worked. We don't know how many vibe founding attempts fail quietly.

The [full paper](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18796231) documents these limitations in detail, including a taxonomy of tasks AI could and couldn't handle.

## This Is Just the Beginning

Two weeks ago, ClawSouls was a concept doc. Today it's a live platform with users, published packages, filed trademarks, and a growing community.

We didn't do this because we're particularly fast or talented. We did it because AI amplifies a small team's execution bandwidth beyond what was previously possible — and this shift is happening right now, in real time.

Vibe coding was the first chapter. Vibe founding is the book.

If you're building something and feel stuck in the gap between vision and execution, find your AI partner. Develop the rhythm. Trust the vibe.

Then ship everything.
